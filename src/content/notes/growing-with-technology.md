The tools I started with are not the tools I use now, and that's the whole point.

I began with the fundamentals at the University of Nigeria: variables, loops, and the slow realisation that software is just a long list of decisions someone had to make. Then Flutter arrived in my life and everything sped up. One codebase, apps on Android and iOS, something on my own phone I could hand to a friend. Campipal and Bailey were built on that foundation, and so was a lot of confidence I hadn't earned yet.

The apps needed data, so I learned backends: Firebase, Node.js, PostgreSQL and Serverpod, which let me stay in Dart from the screen all the way to the database. Being one of the backend developers on Bitwyre showed me what that layer looks like when real money depends on it. Contributing to Wealthfolio showed me how a codebase grows when strangers maintain it together in the open.

Then AI changed the ground under everyone. Working on Genspark AI, an app where people reach models like GPT, Claude and Gemini in one place, I saw how fast the idea of an "app" was shifting. My own Voice Detection experiment was small, but it taught me more about audio and models than any article did. Tools like n8n showed me that automation is often the most useful AI of all: the boring task that simply stops needing a human.

I've tried to learn properly rather than just keep up. The AI Fluency course from Anthropic, Google's AI Professional certificate, IBM's Generative AI Engineering and my certificate from Learn KTS each filled a different gap: how to work with these systems, how to build with them, and when not to use them at all.

But the most important thing I learned recently wasn't a framework. Building websites for Kenz Sushi, Yulmaz and The 11th Floor taught me that the newest technology is rarely what a business needs. They needed their menu online, a booking button that works, and a site that feels like them. The skill was listening, then choosing the simplest tool that does the job well.

That's how I want to keep growing. Not collecting tools like trophies, but building a toolbox deep enough that I can reach for the right one without thinking about it.

Technology never stands still, and neither do I. But I've stopped asking "what's new?" first. Now I ask "what's useful?"
