Great ideas don't need permission from a specific zip code. I have to keep reminding myself of that, because the world doesn't always say it back.

When I started writing code at the University of Nigeria, almost every product I admired was built somewhere else. The tutorials assumed fast internet. The pricing pages were in dollars. The success stories came from cities I had only seen in pictures. It was easy to believe the real work happened far away, and that my job was to watch.

The numbers tell a different story. GitHub's Octoverse report named Nigeria's developer community the fastest-growing in the world in 2023, up 45% in a single year. I'm one of those developers. So is everyone I learned alongside, staying up late on the same Flutter errors and stretching the same data bundles.

What changed me wasn't a report, though. It was shipping. Campipal was a campus app built by someone who knew exactly what campus life here feels like. Elves Meet taught me that video calling on an unstable network is a real engineering problem, not a footnote.

Then the work started coming from further away. I built features for the Genspark AI app as a Flutter developer. I was one of the backend developers on Bitwyre, a trading platform where speed and security are not optional. I contributed to Wealthfolio in the open, where nobody reviewing a pull request cares which country it came from.

The restaurant websites made it concrete. Kenz Sushi and Yulmaz in Algiers. The 11th Floor in South Africa. Real businesses on this continent, with real customers looking for them on their phones. I didn't need to move anywhere to help them. I needed to understand what they were trying to do, and build it well.

That's the part I want to hold on to. Building from Africa isn't a disadvantage I'm working around. It's a vantage point. I know what it's like when the network drops halfway through a payment, when a phone is two years past its best, when a business runs everything through WhatsApp. That knowledge shows up in what I build, and it makes the product better everywhere, not just here.

I don't only want to contribute to global tech. I want to help lead it, with work that holds up next to anything, built from exactly where I am.

The address on my work says Nigeria. The standard it answers to is the world.
