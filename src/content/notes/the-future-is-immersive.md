There is a moment in AR when something digital sits on a real table and stays there. I've never stopped finding it a little bit magic.

I felt that properly while building Genie, my AR app. Getting an object to sit still is harder than it looks. The phone has to understand the floor, the light, the distance, and keep all of it steady while your hand shakes. When it works, you forget about the technology completely. That forgetting is the whole goal.

Augmented reality isn't a novelty anymore. Pokémon Go put creatures on real streets back in 2016. A year later IKEA Place let people drop true-to-size furniture into their living rooms before buying it. Google Maps Live View now paints walking arrows onto the street in front of you. None of these replace the world. They add a layer to it, and that's exactly what I love about AR: it blends the digital and the physical instead of asking you to pick one.

Building Elves Meet taught me a related lesson from a different direction. A video call is immersive in its own way. When it's smooth, you feel close to someone far away. When it lags, you're suddenly aware of every mile between you. Immersion isn't about headsets. It's about how completely the technology gets out of the way.

AI is pushing the same way. With Genspark AI, people type what they want in plain language and get back slides or a document. The interface is starting to disappear into the conversation. I think the next few years will be less about screens we tap and more about experiences that understand what we're looking at and what we mean.

The restaurant sites keep me grounded while I dream. A website for The 11th Floor can't let you taste the food, but it can make you feel the rooftop before you arrive: the gallery, the live music, the light at dinner time. Sometimes immersive just means making someone feel a place before they've been there. I'd love to take that further one day, with a menu you can see in 3D on your own table before you order.

I'm excited to keep building in this space, but I want to build it the right way. Not spectacle for its own sake. Useful magic, the kind that helps people decide, learn, shop or connect a little better.

The best technology doesn't pull you out of the world. It helps you see more of it.
