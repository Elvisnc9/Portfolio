The hardest thing I build is the thing that looks easy.

Dieter Rams, the designer behind so much of Braun, summed up his philosophy in three words: less, but better. I used to read that as a style, clean lines and lots of white space. Now I read it as a discipline. Every button, every option, every extra sentence costs the person using it a little bit of attention, and attention is the one thing nobody has spare.

There's even a law for it. Hick's law says the more choices you give someone, the longer it takes them to decide. I see it every time I strip a screen back. Fewer paths, faster decisions, happier people.

Blail pushed that idea to the edge. It's an email app you can use hands-free, so the simplest interface turned out to be almost no interface at all. The work went into what happens when you speak, not into what you see.

Genspark AI works the same way at a much bigger scale. Behind it sit several powerful AI models, and all the user has to do is describe what they want. The complexity is still there. It's just been carried by the product so the person doesn't have to carry it.

The restaurant sites made simplicity practical. Kenz Sushi's home page asks you to do two things: see the menu, or find the restaurant. Yulmaz puts its restaurant menu and catering menu side by side, one tap each. On every site, "Book a table" stays within reach. A hungry person on a phone doesn't want to explore. They want an answer, and then dinner.

Aurelle was where I practised the craft of it on the frontend: spacing, rhythm, type, the details that make a layout feel calm without anyone knowing why. Nobody notices good spacing. Everybody notices bad spacing.

What I've learned is that simple is not the same as easy. Simple takes more decisions, not fewer. It means saying no to features I'd enjoy building. It means rewriting a sentence until it's half as long. It means testing until the obvious path is really obvious.

I value thoughtful details, clear experiences, and technology that feels natural in someone's hands. When I get it right, nobody says "what a simple design". They just use it and get on with their day.

Simple isn't the absence of work. It's the evidence of it.
